# Switzerland — Tax, Compliance & Visa Intelligence

## Income Tax System
Switzerland has a **three-layer federal structure**: federal, cantonal, and communal taxes. Effective rates vary significantly by canton.

### Federal Income Tax (2024)
- Up to CHF 17,800: 0%
- CHF 17,801–CHF 31,600: 0.77%
- CHF 31,601–CHF 41,400: 0.88%
- Progressive up to **11.5%** for income above CHF 755,200

### Cantonal Tax (varies dramatically)
- **Zug**: ~22% total (federal + cantonal + communal) — lowest in Switzerland
- **Zurich**: ~30–33% total
- **Geneva**: ~35–40% total
- **Basel**: ~32–36% total

### Effective Total Tax Burden (Zurich, CHF 150,000 income)
Approximately 28–32% combined (federal + cantonal + communal). No social security contributions for high earners comparable to France.

## Lump-Sum Taxation (Pauschalbesteuerung)
Available to wealthy non-working foreigners in some cantons:
- Tax based on estimated cost of living (5× annual rent minimum)
- Not available to Swiss nationals or those working in Switzerland

## Double Taxation Agreements (DTA)
Switzerland has DTAs with over 100 countries.

### DTA with India
Switzerland-India DTA (1994):
- Salary: taxed where work is performed
- Dividends: WHT 10%
- Interest: WHT 10%
- Royalties: WHT 10%

### DTA with USA
Switzerland-US DTA (1996):
- US citizens still owe US taxes with Foreign Tax Credit relief
- Banking secrecy significantly reduced since FATCA (2014)

## Work Visa / Permit System

### EU/EFTA Citizens
Freedom of movement within EU/EFTA applies. Registration at commune required.

### Non-EU/EFTA (e.g., Indians, Americans)
Subject to annual quotas. Categories:
- **Permit B (Residence Permit)**: 1-year renewable, requires employer sponsorship
- **Permit C (Settlement Permit)**: After 5–10 years of legal residence (5 for EU, 10 for non-EU)
- **Permit L (Short-Term)**: Up to 1 year for short-term contracts

### Quota System
Switzerland sets annual quotas for non-EU skilled workers:
- Approximately 4,500 long-term permits (B) and 6,500 short-term permits (L) for non-EU per year
- Priority given to specialized roles: IT, finance, biotech, pharma

## Social Security (AHV/AVS)
- Old-age insurance (AHV): 5.3% employee + 5.3% employer
- Disability insurance (IV): 0.7% employee + 0.7% employer
- Unemployment insurance (ALV): 1.1% employee + 1.1% employer (on income up to CHF 148,200)
Total employee social security contributions: approximately 7.1%

## Pension System (Three-Pillar System)
- **Pillar 1 (AHV)**: State pension, mandatory
- **Pillar 2 (BVG)**: Occupational pension, mandatory for employees (both contribute)
- **Pillar 3 (Private)**: Voluntary, tax-deductible savings

## VAT (MWST)
- Standard rate: 8.1%
- Reduced rate: 2.6% (food, books, medicine)
- Special rate: 3.8% (hotel accommodation)

## Banking
Switzerland has a world-class banking system. Swiss banking secrecy has been reduced but still applies to domestic matters.
Major banks: UBS, Credit Suisse (now merged with UBS), Julius Baer, Raiffeisen.
Bank account opening requires residence permit and proof of address.

## Cost-of-Living Context
Switzerland is the most expensive country in Europe:
- 1-bedroom apartment in central Zurich: CHF 2,500–3,500/month (~$2,700–$3,800 USD)
- Meal at restaurant: CHF 20–40 for a basic meal
- Monthly public transport pass: CHF 90–150 depending on zone
- Grocery costs approximately 50–80% higher than UK or Germany
