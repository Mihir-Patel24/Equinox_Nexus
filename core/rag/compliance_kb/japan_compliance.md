# Japan — Tax, Compliance & Visa Intelligence

## Income Tax System — 2024 Rates
Japan has progressive national income tax plus a flat residence tax:

### National Income Tax
- Up to JPY 1,950,000: 5%
- JPY 1,950,001–3,300,000: 10%
- JPY 3,300,001–6,950,000: 20%
- JPY 6,950,001–9,000,000: 23%
- JPY 9,000,001–18,000,000: 33%
- JPY 18,000,001–40,000,000: 40%
- Above JPY 40,000,000: 45%

**Surtax**: 2.1% of income tax (復興特別所得税 — reconstruction surcharge through 2037)

### Residence Tax (Jūminzei)
- Flat 10% on previous year's income (paid via payroll starting June)
- Split: 6% prefectural + 4% municipal

### Social Insurance Contributions (Employee)
- Health insurance (健康保険): approximately 5% (varies by Kenpo)
- Pension (厚生年金): 9.15%
- Employment insurance (雇用保険): 0.6%
- Nursing care (介護保険): 0.9% (age 40+)
Total employee: approximately 15–16%

### Effective Total Tax Burden
Income JPY 10,000,000/year: approximately 36–42% combined

## Special Expatriate Provision
Foreign source income of new residents (non-permanent residents — those who have not been tax resident for 5 of the past 10 years) is taxable in Japan only if remitted to Japan. This creates a significant tax planning opportunity during the first 5 years.

## Double Taxation Agreements (DTA)
Japan has DTAs with 78 countries.

### DTA with India
Japan-India DTA (1989):
- Employment income: taxed where work performed
- Dividends: WHT 10%
- Interest: WHT 10%
- Royalties: WHT 10%

## Work Visas

### Engineer/Specialist in Humanities/International Services (エンジニア/人文知識・国際業務)
Most common work visa for tech and professional workers:
- Requires degree or 10+ years experience in related field
- Employer sponsor required

### Highly Skilled Professional (高度専門職)
Points-based preferential visa:
- Points: academic background, career, age, salary, innovation
- 70+ points: HSP-1 (employer-tied, 3 years)
- 80+ points: HSP-2 (not employer-tied, permanent residency in 1 year eligible)
- Accelerated permanent residency pathway (1 or 3 years instead of 10)

### Startup Visa (特定活動46号 for startup founders)
Available in select municipalities. Allows setting up business without immediate revenue.

## National Health Insurance (NHI) vs. Employer Insurance
- All residents must be enrolled
- Employer-provided: premium shared; typically better coverage
- NHI (self-employed/unemployed): flat premium by municipality

## Consumption Tax (消費税)
- Standard rate: 10%
- Reduced rate: 8% (food and non-alcoholic beverages; take-away)

## Cost-of-Living Context
- Tokyo 1-bedroom central: JPY 120,000–200,000/month (~$800–$1,300 USD)
- Osaka, Fukuoka: 20–30% cheaper than Tokyo
- World-class public transport (Suica card); monthly passes: JPY 10,000–20,000
- Japan is NOT as expensive as commonly perceived; quality-of-life-to-cost ratio is excellent
- Strong food culture; eating out can be affordable (JPY 500–1,500 for full meals)
