# Canada — Tax, Compliance & Visa Intelligence

## Income Tax System — 2024 Federal Rates
Canada has both federal and provincial income taxes:

### Federal Tax Brackets
- Up to CAD 55,867: 15%
- CAD 55,868–111,733: 20.5%
- CAD 111,734–154,906: 26%
- CAD 154,907–220,000: 29%
- Above CAD 220,000: 33%

### Provincial Tax (examples)
- **Ontario**: 5.05%–13.16% provincial tax (combined federal+provincial top rate: ~53%)
- **British Columbia**: 5.06%–20.5% provincial tax
- **Alberta**: Flat 10% provincial tax (no provincial surtax — most tax-friendly province)
- **Quebec**: 14%–25.75% provincial tax (highest in Canada)

### CPP/EI Contributions
- Canada Pension Plan (CPP): 5.95% up to CAD 73,200 earnings
- Employment Insurance (EI): 1.66% up to CAD 63,200

### Effective Total Tax Burden
Income CAD 120,000/year in Ontario: approximately 37–42% combined

## Double Taxation Agreements (DTA)
Canada has DTAs with 93 countries.

### DTA with India
Canada-India DTA (1985, updated):
- Employment income: taxed where work performed
- Dividends: WHT 15% (25% if holding ≥25%)
- Interest: WHT 15%
- Royalties: WHT 15%

## Work Visas

### Express Entry
Primary pathway for skilled workers:
- **Federal Skilled Worker (FSW)**: Points-based system (education, language, experience, age)
- **Canadian Experience Class (CEC)**: For those with Canadian work experience
- **Federal Skilled Trades (FST)**: For certified tradespeople

### Intra-Company Transfer (ICT)
For employees of multinational companies transferring to Canadian affiliate.

### Global Talent Stream (GTS)
Expedited 2-week processing for highly skilled tech workers. Employer must apply for Labour Market Impact Assessment (LMIA) exemption.

### Provincial Nominee Programs (PNPs)
Each province nominates workers based on local labour market needs. Popular:
- Ontario Immigrant Nominee Program (OINP)
- BC PNP Tech
- Alberta Advantage Immigration Program (AAIP)

## Healthcare
Canada has universal healthcare (Medicare) funded by taxes. Provinces administer it:
- Most provinces have a 3-month wait for new residents before coverage begins
- Prescription drugs NOT covered by provincial health — supplemental insurance recommended

## GST/HST
- Federal GST: 5%
- HST (combined federal + provincial): Ontario 13%, BC 12%, Quebec 14.975%
- Alberta, Saskatchewan: 5% GST only (no provincial sales tax)

## Cost-of-Living Context
- Toronto 1-bedroom central: CAD 2,200–3,200/month (~$1,600–$2,400 USD)
- Vancouver: similar to Toronto
- Calgary: 20–30% cheaper than Toronto
- Montreal: 30–40% cheaper than Toronto (but Quebec taxes are higher)
- Monthly transit pass (Toronto TTC): CAD 156/month
