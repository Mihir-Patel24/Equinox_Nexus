# Germany — Tax, Compliance & Visa Intelligence

## Income Tax System — 2024 Rates
Germany uses a progressive formula-based tax rate (not fixed brackets):
- €0–€11,604: 0% (basic allowance)
- €11,605–€66,760: 14%–42% (progressive curve)
- €66,761–€277,825: 42%
- Above €277,826: 45% (Reichensteuer / wealth surcharge)

**Solidarity Surcharge (Soli)**: 5.5% of income tax for incomes above €35,827 (significantly reduced since 2021)
**Church Tax (Kirchensteuer)**: 8–9% of income tax (optional; applies if registered with a church)

### Social Security Contributions (Employee) — 2024
- Pension insurance (RV): 9.3%
- Health insurance (KV): 7.3% + additional contribution ~1.6% (employer pays equal share)
- Unemployment insurance (AV): 1.3%
- Care insurance (PV): 1.7% (childless: +0.6% = 2.3%)
Total employee social contributions: approximately 20–22%

### Effective Total Tax Burden
Income €80,000/year: approximately 48–52% combined (income tax + social contributions)

## Work Visas

### EU Blue Card
- For highly skilled non-EU workers
- Minimum salary: €45,552/year (2024) general; €41,042 for shortage occupations (IT, engineering, medicine)
- Fast-track 2-week processing common

### Skilled Immigration Act (Fachkräfteeinwanderungsgesetz) — 2023 Reform
Germany reformed immigration law in 2023, creating new pathways:
- **Skilled worker visa**: formal qualification + job offer; no salary minimum if occupation in shortage list
- **Opportunity Card (Chancenkarte)**: Points-based visa for job search (6–12 months), without prior job offer
- **Recognition of Foreign Qualifications**: Streamlined process for verifying non-EU credentials

### Job Seeker Visa
- 6-month visa to search for work within Germany
- Requires: university degree recognized in Germany + 5 years experience + sufficient funds

## Healthcare
Germany has a dual healthcare system:
- **Statutory (GKV)**: Mandatory for income below €69,300/year; ~14.6% + supplement (employee pays half)
- **Private (PKV)**: Optional for high earners; often more comprehensive, but more expensive

## Pension System
Germany's statutory pension (Deutsche Rentenversicherung) requires 5 years of contributions for entitlement. Expats leaving Germany can receive a refund of pension contributions after 24 months if their home country has no bilateral pension agreement.

## VAT (Mehrwertsteuer / Umsatzsteuer)
- Standard rate: 19%
- Reduced rate: 7% (food, books, public transport, art)
- Zero rate: 0% (exports)
