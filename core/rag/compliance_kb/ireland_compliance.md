# Ireland — Tax, Compliance & Visa Intelligence

## Income Tax System — 2024 Rates
Ireland has a two-band system with Universal Social Charge (USC) on top:

### Income Tax
- Standard Rate: 20% on income up to €42,000 (single)
- Higher Rate: 40% on income above €42,000

### Universal Social Charge (USC)
- 0.5% on first €12,012
- 2% on €12,013–€25,760
- 4% on €25,761–€70,044
- 8% on €70,045+

### PRSI (Pay Related Social Insurance)
- Employee: 4% (increasing to 4.1% from October 2024)
- Employer: 11.15%

### Effective Total Tax Burden
Income €80,000/year: approximately 44–48% combined (income tax + USC + PRSI)

## Special Assignee Relief Programme (SARP)
For employees assigned to Ireland by foreign employers:
- 30% of income above €100,000 exempt from income tax
- Duration: up to 5 years
- Eligibility: not resident in Ireland for 5 prior years; must earn €75,000+

## Double Taxation Agreements (DTA)
Ireland has DTAs with 75+ countries.

### DTA with India
Ireland-India DTA (2001):
- Employment income: taxed where work performed
- Dividends: WHT 10%
- Interest: WHT 10%
- Royalties: WHT 10%

## Work Visas

### Critical Skills Employment Permit (CSEP)
- For roles on Critical Skills Occupation List (IT, engineering, finance, healthcare)
- Minimum salary: €32,000/year (list occupations); €64,000/year (non-list)
- 2 years, renewable; path to permanent residency

### General Employment Permit
- For roles not on critical skills list
- Minimum salary: €34,000/year
- Employer must first try to hire from EEA

### Stamp 1G (Graduate Visa)
- For Irish university graduates; 12–24 months to seek employment

## Digital Nomad Visa
Ireland does not currently have a dedicated digital nomad visa program (as of 2024).

## VAT
- Standard rate: 23%
- Reduced rate: 13.5% (tourism, catering, hotel services)
- Reduced rate: 9% (news publications, sports facilities — restored 2024)
- Zero rate: 0% (food, children's clothing, oral medicines)

## Cost-of-Living Context
- Dublin 1-bedroom central: €2,000–€3,000/month (extreme housing shortage)
- Cork, Galway: 15–25% cheaper than Dublin but still expensive
- Monthly Leap Card (Dublin): approximately €140/month
- Ireland is the second most expensive country in EU for consumer prices (2024)
- Strong tech sector (Google, Meta, Microsoft, Apple European HQs in Dublin)
