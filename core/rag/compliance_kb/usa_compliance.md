# United States of America — Tax, Compliance & Visa Intelligence

## Federal Income Tax
The US has a progressive federal income tax system with filing statuses (Single, Married Filing Jointly, Head of Household, etc.).

### 2024 Federal Tax Brackets (Single Filer)
- $0–$11,600: 10%
- $11,601–$47,150: 12%
- $47,151–$100,525: 22%
- $100,526–$191,950: 24%
- $191,951–$243,725: 32%
- $243,726–$609,350: 35%
- Above $609,350: 37%

Standard deduction (2024): $14,600 (Single), $29,200 (MFJ)

### State Income Tax
Varies significantly by state:
- **No state income tax**: Texas, Florida, Nevada, Washington, Wyoming, South Dakota, Alaska, Tennessee, New Hampshire (dividends/interest only)
- **Low state tax**: North Dakota, Arizona, Indiana (~3–5%)
- **Medium state tax**: Colorado, Michigan, Pennsylvania (3–5%)
- **High state tax**: California (up to 13.3%), New York City (up to 3.876% city + 10.9% state), New Jersey (up to 10.75%), Oregon (up to 9.9%)

For New York City specifically: federal (~35%) + state (~10.9%) + city (~3.876%) = **~50% total marginal rate** at high incomes.

## FICA (Social Security + Medicare)
- Social Security: 6.2% employee (up to wage base $168,600 in 2024)
- Medicare: 1.45% employee (no wage cap)
- Additional Medicare: 0.9% on wages above $200,000 (single)
- Employer matches Social Security and Medicare (not the additional 0.9%)

**Total employee FICA: 7.65% up to wage base, then 1.45% + 0.9%**

## State/City-Specific Examples

### New York City (Manhattan)
Federal + State + City total marginal rate at $150,000: ~42–44%
Effective total rate on $150,000: ~35–38%

### Texas (Austin, Houston, Dallas)
Federal only. No state or city income tax.
Effective total rate on $150,000: ~25–28% (federal only)

### California (San Francisco, Los Angeles)
California state tax up to 13.3% (for income above $1M; 9.3% for income $61,215–$312,686)
Effective total rate on $150,000: ~33–38%

## Foreign Earned Income Exclusion (FEIE)
US citizens and Green Card holders living abroad can exclude up to **$126,500 (2024)** of foreign earned income from US taxation if they meet the bona fide residence test or physical presence test (330 days outside US in 12-month period).

## Double Taxation Agreements (DTA)
The US has DTAs with ~68 countries. The US DTA does NOT override the US obligation to tax citizens on worldwide income, but provides Foreign Tax Credits.

### DTA with India
The India-US DTAA provides treaty benefits on:
- Employment income (taxed in country of performance)
- Dividends: 15–25% WHT
- Interest: 15% WHT
- Royalties: 10–15% WHT
Indian nationals in the US on H-1B visa are subject to full US tax. India may also tax if they are Indian tax resident.

## Visa Categories

### H-1B Visa — Specialty Occupation
- Most common work visa for professionals
- Requires sponsoring employer, bachelor's degree (or equivalent) in specialty field
- Annual cap: 65,000 regular + 20,000 for US master's degree holders
- Duration: 3 years, extendable to 6 years
- Lottery applies (oversubscribed): ~30% selection rate in recent years
- H-1B holders can apply for Green Card (EB-2, EB-3); Indian nationals face severe backlogs (10–50+ years due to country per-country limits)

### L-1 Visa — Intracompany Transferee
- For managers (L-1A) or specialized knowledge workers (L-1B) transferred within same company
- Requires 1 year of continuous employment at foreign affiliate in last 3 years
- L-1A: 7 years max; L-1B: 5 years max
- L-1A provides faster path to EB-1C Green Card

### O-1 Visa — Extraordinary Ability
- For individuals with extraordinary ability in sciences, arts, education, business, or athletics
- Evidence of awards, high salary, peer recognition required
- No annual cap

### E-3 Visa — Australian Nationals Only
- For Australian citizens in specialty occupations
- Annual cap: 10,500; heavily underutilized

### TN Visa — Canadian/Mexican Nationals (USMCA)
- For specific professions listed in USMCA annex (engineers, accountants, scientists, etc.)
- Admitted at port of entry; no petition required for Canadians

## Green Card (Permanent Residency)
Employment-based categories:
- **EB-1**: Priority workers (extraordinary ability, outstanding professors, multinational managers) — no labor certification, fastest
- **EB-2**: Advanced degree or exceptional ability — labor certification (PERM) generally required; NIW (National Interest Waiver) exception
- **EB-3**: Skilled workers and professionals — labor certification required; longer backlogs

**Critical Note for Indian Nationals**: Due to the per-country annual limit on Green Cards (7%), Indian nationals in EB-2 and EB-3 categories face backlogs of **20–60+ years** as of 2024. EB-1 and EB-1C have much shorter waits (months to ~2 years for India).

## Social Security Benefits
Foreign nationals who work in the US and pay FICA taxes may be eligible for Social Security benefits at retirement age if they accumulate 40 quarters (10 years) of covered earnings. The US has Totalization Agreements with 30+ countries to prevent double contributions.

## 401(k) Retirement Plans
Employer-sponsored defined contribution plan. 2024 contribution limit: $23,000 ($30,500 if 50+). Many employers match contributions (commonly 50% of first 6% of salary = 3% of salary free match). Pre-tax or Roth (post-tax) options.
